## Key Findings

- **Campaign response:** 334 of 2,240 customers responded to the campaign, resulting in a **14.91% response rate**.

![Campaign Response Rate](visualizations/campaign_response.png)
> **Insight:** The marketing campaign successfully engaged 14.91% of the customer base, forming a solid core segment for targeted retention.

---

- **Purchase channels:** Store purchases had the highest total volume (**12,970**), followed by Web (**9,150**) and Catalog (**5,963**).

![Total Purchases by Channel](visualizations/purchases_by_channel.png)
> **Insight:** Physical stores remain the primary sales driver, while web channels contribute significantly, showing a strong omnichannel presence.

---

- **Income segments:** Based on the defined income segments, the **Medium Income** group was the largest (**1,267 customers**), while the High Income segment had an average income of **$90.8K**.

---

- **Customer profile:** Customers without children had a higher average income (**$65.7K**) than customers with children (**$46.9K**).

---

- **Education:** Average income varied across education levels, with PhD customers averaging **$56.1K** compared with **$20.3K** for customers with Basic education.

![Average Income by Education Level](visualizations/income_by_education.png)
> **Insight:** Average income varies across education levels, with PhD and Master’s degree holders among the higher-income groups.

---

- **Spending behavior:** The analysis identified the **top 10 customers by total product spending**, providing a basis for high-value customer retention strategies.

---

- **Campaign & digital behavior:** Campaign responders averaged **5.07 web purchases** compared with **3.91** for non-responders, while average monthly web visits were nearly identical (**5.29 vs. 5.32**).

![Digital Behavior](visualizations/digital_behavior.png)
> **Insight:** Responders made more web purchases (5.07 vs 3.91) despite having similar average monthly web visits (5.29 vs 5.32).
